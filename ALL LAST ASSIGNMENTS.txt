Project Plan - Due10:00 AM
Project Plan

Project Plan
Introduction
Ready to turn your database idea into reality? Think of your project plan as a blueprint for success - it's where you'll map out your solution to a real-world problem using SQL Server's powerful tools. Whether you're designing a system to track your local gym's membership or managing inventory for a bustling coffee shop, you'll start by sketching out your database's anatomy through an ERD (Entity Relationship Diagram). This visual roadmap shows how your tables connect and interact, just like creating a family tree for your data!


Instructions
Choose an idea from the list of database project ideas you generated during the brainstorming session.

Develop a project plan to implement your database. Your project plan should include:
Project Description (1 page):

A clear problem statement explaining the current challenges (2-3 sentences)
Your proposed database solution (2-3 sentences)
The types of data you'll need to track (bullet points)
A paragraph addressing database administration concerns:
How will your design ensure scalability as data grows?
What measures will you implement for high availability?
How will you address security requirements?
Database Design:

An Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD) showing all tables and their relationships
Must demonstrate normalization to at least 2NF
Include all primary and foreign key relationships
Show all attributes for each entity
The sample data for each table that you'll use for testing

You can choose the tool you use for your ERD, like draw.io or Eraser.io.

 Prepare a rough draft you can share in class for peer review. You don't need to turn in your rough draft of your project plan. Instead, bring your rough draft to class for the project plan review. 

Use the feedback from your classmates to revise your project plan.

Upload the final project plan to this assignment page.

Project Database - Due10:00 AM
Project DatabaseSQL Server Dancing

Introduction

Welcome to the hands-on phase of your SQL Server project! Now's the exciting part where you'll bring your database design to life. Let's turn those ERD diagrams into a working database!

Instructions
Build your database following these requirements using SQL.

Save your SQL statements frequently as you are working so you can turn them in at the end of the project.

Create a minimum of 8 properly normalized tables (2NF or higher).

Populate the tables with your sample data from your project plan.

Implement 3 stored procedures:
One for inserting or updating data
One for retrieving data in a meaningful way
One for deleting data

Design two views that:
Summarize important business metrics
Join multiple tables to provide useful information

During Sprint #1, we will hold a short stand-up meeting where each student will share: 
What they've completed
What they're working on next
Any blockers or challenges they're facing

During Sprint #2, we will hold a database project review where you can collaborate with peers and get help with challenges.

After the database review, use the feedback you got from your peers to revise your database.

Copy and paste your SQL statements into the textbox at the bottom of this assignment page and click Submit.
Remember to submit SQL statements for creating your database, populating it, creating your stored procedures, and creating your views.

Data Population (16 pts)	Complete sample data matches project plan and demonstrates all relationships.	Sample data present but missing some relationships or scenarios.	Minimal data populated or data inconsistent with plan.	You did not upload the project database to the assignment dropbox by the due date listed on the course schedule.
